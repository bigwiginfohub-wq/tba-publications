## 1. What Is a Self-Audit?

A self-audit is a **disciplined look at your own thinking**. It is not about judging yourself as good or bad. It is about mapping:

- What you believe
- How confident you are
- Where others might disagree
- What would change your mind
- What you do not know

The goal is not to achieve perfect certainty. The goal is to **know what you know, know what you don't know, and know the difference**.

---

## 2. The Four Delta Principles (Plain Language)

| Principle | What It Asks | In One Sentence |
|-----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **H₃ Warrant** | What would prove you wrong? | "I would change my mind if..." |
| **Friction Score** | How much do others disagree? | "On a scale of 0-100, how much would others disagree with me?" |
| **MCL Coefficient** | How confident are you in the cause-and-effect? | "How sure am I that A actually causes B?" |
| **Boundary Statement** | What don't you know? | "I am not claiming X. I do not know Y." |

These four questions are the entire method. You do not need math. You need honesty.

---

## 3. Step 1: Gather Your Trace

Before you audit a specific belief, collect evidence of how you actually think.

| Source | What to Look For |
|--------|------------------|
| **Journals** | Repeated patterns, worries, convictions |
| **Messages (email, chat)** | How you argue, what you assume, how you react to disagreement |
| **Decisions (work, personal)** | What factors you weighed, what you ignored |
| **Feedback from others** | What people say about your thinking (if available) |

Do not judge what you find. Just collect.

---

## 4. Step 2: State Your Claim

Write down one belief, decision, or pattern you want to audit.

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Belief | "Meditation reduces my anxiety." |
| Decision | "I chose Job A over Job B because it offered more flexibility." |
| Pattern | "I tend to avoid conflict in relationships." |

Keep it specific. One sentence.

**Your claim:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

---

## 5. Step 3: Apply H₃ Warrant

Ask: *"What specific, observable evidence would make me change my mind?"*

| Good H₃ | Poor H₃ |
|---------|---------|
| "If three controlled studies showed no effect, I would change my mind." | "If God told me I was wrong." (not observable) |
| "If my boss told me I was avoiding conflict, I would reconsider." | "If I felt different." (vague) |
| "If the new job offered the same flexibility, I would regret my choice." | "If things worked out differently." (not specific) |

Write your H₃ Warrant:

**I would change my mind if:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

---

## 6. Step 4: Estimate Friction Score

Ask: *"If I surveyed 100 knowledgeable people, how many would disagree with my claim?"*

| Friction (D) | Meaning | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| 0.00–0.20 D | Almost everyone agrees | "The Earth orbits the Sun." |
| 0.21–0.40 D | Most agree, some disagree | "Meditation reduces anxiety." |
| 0.41–0.60 D | Significant disagreement | "Inflation happened in the early universe." |
| 0.61–0.80 D | Most disagree | "Aliens have visited Earth." |
| 0.81–1.00 D | Almost everyone disagrees | "The Moon is made of cheese." |

**My friction score (0–1 D):** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

---

## 7. Step 5: Estimate MCL Coefficient

Ask: *"How confident am I that this cause leads to this effect?"*

| MCL (D) | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| 0.90–1.00 D | Extremely confident, well-established | "Smoking causes lung cancer." |
| 0.70–0.89 D | Confident, good evidence, some uncertainty | "Meditation reduces anxiety." |
| 0.50–0.69 D | Moderately confident, plausible, not proven | "Social media causes teen depression." |
| 0.30–0.49 D | Low confidence, weak evidence | "Astrology predicts personality." |
| 0.00–0.29 D | Very low confidence, speculative | "Crystals heal diseases." |

**My MCL coefficient (0–1 D):** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

---

## 8. Step 6: Write Your Boundary Statement

Ask: *"What am I not claiming? What do I not know?"*

| Good Boundary | Poor Boundary |
|---------------|----------------|
| "I am not claiming that meditation works for everyone. I do not know the long-term effects." | "I could be wrong." (trivial) |
| "I do not know how the other job would have turned out. I am not claiming it was the objectively correct choice." | "Nothing is certain." (vague) |
| "I have not measured my anxiety objectively. I am not claiming clinical improvement." | No boundary statement. |

Write your Boundary Statement:

**I am not claiming:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

**I do not know:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

## 9. Step 7: Calculate Your Integrity Score (Optional)

If you want a single number, use this formula:

```
Integrity Score (D) = (0.30 × H₃) + (0.25 × (1 - Friction)) + (0.25 × MCL) + (0.20 × Boundary)
```

Where:

- **H₃** = 1.00 if your H₃ Warrant is specific and testable; 0.50 if vague; 0.00 if none
- **Friction** = your friction score (0–1 D)
- **MCL** = your MCL coefficient (0–1 D)
- **Boundary** = 1.00 if you stated at least two meaningful boundaries; 0.50 if one; 0.00 if none

**Example:**

| Component | Score | Weight | Contribution |
|-----------|-------|--------|--------------|
| H₃ | 1.00 | 30% | 0.30 |
| 1 - Friction (0.30) | 0.70 | 25% | 0.175 |
| MCL (0.70) | 0.70 | 25% | 0.175 |
| Boundary | 1.00 | 20% | 0.20 |
| **Total** | | | **0.85 D (85 D)** |

**My Integrity Score:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

## 10. Step 8: Document and Revisit

| Action | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Save your audit (notebook, file, note app) | So you can compare later |
| Date it | Track changes over time |
| Revisit in 3–6 months | See how your thinking has evolved |
| Update your scores | Growth is not failure |

**Optional:** Share your audit with a trusted person. Ask them: "Do you agree with my friction score? Do you see boundaries I missed?"

## 11. Example: Completed Self-Audit

**Claim:** "Meditation reduces my anxiety."

| Step | Response |
|------|----------|
| **H₃ Warrant** | If three weeks of daily meditation showed no change in my self-reported anxiety (1-10 scale), I would change my mind. |
| **Friction Score** | 0.30 D (most people agree, but some say it depends on the person) |
| **MCL Coefficient** | 0.70 D (plausible, I have experienced it, but not clinically measured) |
| **Boundary Statement** | I am not claiming it works for everyone. I do not know the long-term effects. I have not measured objectively. |
| **Integrity Score** | H₃=1.00, Friction=0.30, MCL=0.70, Boundary=1.00 → **0.85 D (85 D)** |
