## What Newton Discovered

### 1. The Trinity is a Corruption

Newton's manuscript *Paradoxical Questions concerning the Morals and Actions of Athanasius* (c. 1690s) argued that Athanasius, the 4th-century bishop, introduced the Trinity doctrine for power, not for truth. Newton called him a "fraud" and a "litigator."

He also analyzed 1 John 5:7 (the *Comma Johanneum*), demonstrating through manuscript evidence that the Trinitarian formula was a later addition. Modern textual scholarship has vindicated him.

### 2. The Original Faith Was Pure Monotheism

Newton believed the earliest Christians — before the Council of Nicaea (325 AD) — worshipped one God, the Father, and revered Jesus as His Messiah, not as God Himself. He sought to restore this "primitive" faith.

### 3. The Bible Had Been Altered

Newton's *Historical Account of Two Notable Corruptions of Scripture* detailed specific verses that were added or altered. He concluded that the original revelation had been distorted by human hands.

## Why Newton Stopped

| Reason | Explanation |
|--------|-------------|
| **Fear of prosecution** | A friend lost his Cambridge professorship for denying the Trinity. An 18-year-old student was hanged in Edinburgh in 1697 for the same offense. |
| **Loss of position** | Newton valued his role at Cambridge and the Mint. He was not willing to sacrifice them. |
| **No preserved core** | Newton had no uncorrupted scripture to serve as a criterion. He could only try to reconstruct an imagined original. |
| **No method** | He had textual criticism and historical forensics, but no falsifiable framework for testing claims. |

Newton hid his theological manuscripts. They remained unpublished for centuries. He published his *Observations on Daniel* and *Chronology* posthumously, but his explicitly anti-Trinitarian works were never released in his lifetime.
