\## Abstract



The Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ night journey (Isra and Mi'raj) describes a creature, Al-Buraq, capable of terrestrial and celestial travel at extraordinary speed. This paper analyzes the Buraq's described features through both classical Islamic scholarship and modern physics. The conclusion: the Buraq is not a mythical creature but a \*\*vehicle operating on principles of coherent gravity amplification\*\* — principles that are mathematically describable, physically testable, and fully compatible with tawhid (divine oneness). The miracle is not a violation of physics but an \*\*application of physics at a level humans had not yet discovered\*\*.



\---



\## 1. The Source Texts



Primary sources (Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim) describe:



\- A white creature, smaller than a mule but larger than a donkey

\- "Its stride covered the farthest boundary of one's gaze"

\- \*\*Two wings on its thighs\*\* that "lent strength to its legs"

\- Travel from Mecca to Jerusalem (≈1,200 km) in "a part of the night"

\- Ascension through the seven heavens



The name \*\*Al-Buraq\*\* (الْبُرَاق) derives from \*barq\* (lightning), indicating light emission, extreme speed, or electrical/plasma phenomena.

\## 3. Physical Interpretation (Conceptual Summary)



\### 3.1 Speed and Relativity



The Buraq's ability to cover vast distances in a short subjective time is consistent with \*\*time dilation\*\* — a proven phenomenon of special and general relativity. The Prophet's experience of the journey was real; the physical time elapsed on Earth was also real. The difference is explained by relativistic travel at speeds approaching light.



\*\*Quranic alignment:\*\* "A day with your Lord is like a thousand years of your reckoning" (Quran 22:47). The Prophet experienced the Mi'raj in a single night; Earth experienced the same night. Time dilation does not contradict the text; it explains it.



\### 3.2 The Two Wings



The Buraq's wings are described on its \*\*thighs\*\*, not its back — an anatomically implausible location for biological flight. This indicates the wings are not for aerodynamic lift but for \*\*stabilization and amplification\*\* of the primary propulsion (the "legs").



\*\*Modern parallel:\*\* Spacecraft use deployable solar panels and antennae that "unfold" after launch. These are not for flight; they are for \*\*function\*\*. The Buraq's wings serve a similar purpose — they "lend strength" by increasing the vehicle's interaction with the ambient gravitational field.



\### 3.3 Coherent Freefall



The Buraq's movement is described as \*smooth\* and \*without vibration\* — consistent with \*\*coherent freefall\*\*. The vehicle is not fighting gravity; it is \*\*falling with it\*\*. The sensation of falling is not experienced as violent acceleration; it is experienced as smooth motion.



\*\*Quranic alignment:\*\* "The sun and the moon follow a reckoning" (Quran 55:5). All celestial bodies are in coherent freefall. The Buraq is no different — it simply achieves this state intentionally.
