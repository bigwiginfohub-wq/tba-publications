## Abstract

The dys (symbol: D) is a proposed unit of measurement for **disciplined agreement** — a quantitative expression of how much confidence can be placed in a claim, score, or assessment based on transparency, reproducibility, and consensus. Unlike physical units (meter, second, kilogram), the dys does not measure a property of the universe. It measures a property of **human understanding** — specifically, the disciplined process of testing, debating, and agreeing on claims.

This paper defines the dys, its relationship to the Delta principles (H₃ Warrant, Friction Score, MCL Coefficient, Boundary Statement), its calibration, and its potential applications in science, governance, AI auditing, and everyday reasoning.

---

## 1. Introduction

Human beings make claims constantly. Some claims are tested empirically. Others are debated philosophically. Many are asserted with confidence that far exceeds the available evidence. The result is confusion, conflict, and the erosion of trust.

Existing units of measurement (meter, second, kilogram, Kelvin, mole, candela, ampere) measure physical properties of the universe. They do not measure the **quality of agreement** about those properties. Two scientists can agree on the distance to a star (10 light-years) but disagree entirely on the confidence of that measurement. There is no unit for that disagreement.

The dys fills this gap. It provides a common language for expressing:

- How much divergence exists among experts (Friction Score)
- How confident we are in a causal link (MCL Coefficient)
- What would prove a claim wrong (H₃ Warrant)
- What is explicitly not being claimed (Boundary Statement)

The dys is not a physical constant. It is an **epistemic convention** — a tool for disciplined truth-seeking.

## 2. Definition of the Dys

### 2.1 Symbol and Name

- **Symbol:** D
- **Name:** dys (plural: dys)
- **Pronunciation:** /dɪs/ (rhymes with "this")

The name is derived from the Greek *dýnamis* (power, ability, agreement) and *dýo* (two — as in the meeting of minds).

### 2.2 Range

The dys ranges from 0.00 D to 1.00 D (or 0 to 100 D when expressed as a percentage).

| Value | Verbal Equivalent |
|-------|-------------------|
| 0.00 D | No agreement / no confidence |
| 0.50 D | Moderate agreement / moderate confidence |
| 1.00 D | Perfect agreement / absolute confidence (theoretically unattainable) |

In practice, values above 0.95 D are considered "exceptional" and values below 0.30 D are considered "low."

### 2.3 Relation to the Delta Principles

The dys is not a direct measurement. It is a **composite unit** derived from four Delta metrics:

| Delta Metric | Symbol | Range | Contribution to Dys |
|--------------|--------|-------|---------------------|
| Friction Score | D_friction | 0.00–1.00 | Inverse relationship (higher friction = lower dys) |
| MCL Coefficient | D_mcl | 0.00–1.00 | Direct relationship (higher MCL = higher dys) |
| H₃ Warrant | (binary + quality) | 0.00–1.00 | Direct relationship (clear falsifiability = higher dys) |
| Boundary Statement | (quality) | 0.00–1.00 | Direct relationship (clear boundaries = higher dys) |

The dys is typically reported as an **Integrity Score** — a weighted combination of these four metrics. A common weighting is:

```
Integrity Score (D) = (0.30 × H₃_Quality) + (0.25 × (1 - Friction)) + (0.25 × MCL) + (0.20 × Boundary_Quality)
```

Other weightings are possible depending on the domain and purpose.

## 3. Calibration

### 3.1 Anchor Points

The dys is calibrated against observable consensus and empirical validation.

| Dys Value | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 0.95–1.00 D | Exceptional confidence; virtually no divergence; strong causal evidence | The Earth orbits the Sun |
| 0.85–0.94 D | Very high confidence; minimal divergence; well-established | Evolution by natural selection |
| 0.70–0.84 D | High confidence; some divergence; good evidence | Dark matter exists (anomaly is real) |
| 0.50–0.69 D | Moderate confidence; significant divergence; plausible | Cosmic inflation |
| 0.30–0.49 D | Low confidence; high divergence; weak evidence | String theory |
| 0.00–0.29 D | Very low confidence; speculative or unfalsifiable | Multiverse (untestable variants) |

## 4. Applications

### 4.1 Auditing AI Claims

The dys provides a standardized way for AI systems to report confidence. Instead of saying "I am confident," an AI could say: "My confidence is 0.85 D, with a friction score of 0.15 D (low divergence), MCL coefficient of 0.90 D (strong causal evidence), and the following boundary statement: I have not verified the source data."

### 4.2 Scientific Communication

Researchers could report their confidence in findings using dys, enabling meta-analysis and systematic reviews to aggregate not just effect sizes but also confidence distributions.

### 4.3 Policy and Governance

Policymakers could require that claims presented as evidence be accompanied by dys scores, making explicit the level of agreement, the falsifiability conditions, and the boundaries of the claim.

### 4.4 Personal Decision-Making

Individuals could audit their own beliefs using the dys, tracking how their confidence changes over time as they acquire new evidence.

### 4.5 Scripture and Theology

As demonstrated in the Quran Map, the dys can be used to report the alignment between scriptural claims and scientific evidence, without forcing harmony or declaring contradiction.

## 5. Limitations

| Limitation | Explanation |
|------------|-------------|
| **Not a physical constant** | The dys is a convention, not a property of nature. |
| **Subject to calibration disputes** | Different communities may set different anchor points. |
| **Not a substitute for evidence** | The dys reports confidence; it does not generate evidence. |
| **Metaphysical claims** | Claims about the unseen (God, angels, the soul) may have no falsifiable H₃ Warrant. In such cases, the dys cannot be calculated; the appropriate response is a Boundary Statement. |
| **Human bias** | Dys scores are assigned by humans or AI systems; they are not immune to bias. |

## 8. Conclusion

The dys is a proposed unit of disciplined agreement — a tool for expressing confidence, divergence, falsifiability, and humility in a standardized, transparent manner. It is not a physical constant. It is an epistemic convention, designed to improve the quality of human discourse about truth.

The dys does not eliminate disagreement. It **measures** it — so that disagreement can be discussed honestly, without pretending that certainty exists where it does not.

The mirror does not change. But what we measure with it can.
